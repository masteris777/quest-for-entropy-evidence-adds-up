Evidence Adds Up to a Fact

#18 ended on a question: a marker meets, a detector settles; how are they really different? The rules are the ones of the Meeting Game: everything is a particle, every particle is a table, the only move is a meeting. This bench walks the answer claim by claim. Eight rooms; each has presets for the cases worth seeing, and free dials for the rest. The square itself (why the odds are length squared) is the Meeting Game's rooms 1 and 2: open it. To build your own meetings, at any strength, and see how much interference a joint undo restores: the Meeting Sandbox.

In a nutshell

The rules: everything is a particle, every particle is a table, and the only move is a meeting. A settlement is what those meetings add up to. Seven steps:

  1. A meeting makes a promise. Two tables join into one. Both lines are still there; nothing is written. A marker's meeting and a detector's first touch are the same move.room 1
  2. A line is written where two holders match. A meeting with a newcomer copies the promise. A meeting of two holders is a chance: each makes one light touch, which lands on a line with a chance set by its length. If both land on the same line, it is written for every holder. Length times length is the square. Nothing is proposed or refused, and a lone thread that never meets a holder again never writes.room 2
  3. Every later meeting copies. A witness that meets a settled particle finds one line left, so it can only copy. Re-signing would contradict the books; voting would lose the square.room 3
  4. A fact is a line nobody can undo any more. An undo must reach every book that holds the line and reverse the meetings, last first. Its chance falls geometrically with the books.room 4
  5. Only direct looks count. Each direct witness out of reach costs the undo a factor. A chain of retellings costs it only the first link. Evidence adds up; hearsay doesn't.room 5
  6. Where the write sits is invisible. Anyone who misses one book predicts exactly the same readings whether or not a line was written. The table never narrows; narrowing is what you get by conditioning on the books beyond reach.room 6
  7. A fact is a fact for someone. A copy counts only for a reading that could contradict it. A fact inside a closed box is decided but not yet received outside; opening the box delivers it.room 7room 8

Terms (one meaning each, across the page)

table: a particle's list of cells, each with an arrow. Two particles that met share one joint table.
line: one outcome the table could still come to. Stands for: a branch.
length: the size of a line's arrow. turn: a move that shifts length between lines.
cell: an equal piece of a line. A line of length √n is n cells, and its odds are its share of the cells.
meeting: the only move. One table copies the other's line, and the two become one table. Stands for: entanglement (a CNOT).
look: a partial meeting [controlled interaction], turned only part of the way. An untouched witness's look of angle φ leaves a factor cos φ of the original fringe contrast recoverable [interference visibility]; 90° is a full copy.
promise: a joint table where no line is written yet. All its lines are still there.
book: what one particle holds about the outcome. counterparty: all the other books that hold the promise, together. The odds come from the contract: a lone book gets only what symmetry forces, never odds from its lengths; the lengths become odds once a counterparty holds the other side.
holder: a particle that holds the promise. touch: one holder's light contact in a meeting: usually nothing, sometimes one line, with a chance set by its length.
settlement: the first meeting where two holders touch the same line; the line is written for every holder. A miss leaves nothing. Stands for: a measurement outcome.
witness: any particle that meets the settled one later; it can only copy. A copy is what its meeting can do: repeat the line.
crowd / chain: witnesses who each look at the fact directly / who each hear it from the one before.
undo: reversing meetings, last first. reach: which books an undo can touch.
fact: a written line that no undo within reach can take back. Stands for: a classical record.

The particle P, two lines, lengths
room 1

The meeting is the same

measured on the toy
presets

P meets M. That first touch is the same meeting whether M is #18's marker or a particle of a detector: a promise, two lines, nothing written.

The difference comes after. An isolated marker keeps the trace to itself. A detector particle passes it to its neighbours [environment fragments]. Each meeting extends the shared wave; no line is selected here.

Before detecting P, we reverse its joint meeting with M [inverse unitary operation]. This undo reaches P and M, but not the crowd. With no escaped trace, subsequent P readings recover the full fringe. #18 used a different move: turn M, read it, and sort already recorded P results [conditional interference]. That leaves the unsorted results unchanged.

A detector is a marker whose trace spreads. The escaped correlations suppress interference in P's readings alone [decoherence], even after we undo the P–M meeting.

Nothing here picks the line. Room 2 supplies a separate toy rule for choosing an answer; this room tests how much interference a limited undo can recover.

The handshake room: where a line is written

HOW ARE THE TWO TOUCHES PUT TOGETHER?

P has met D: a promise, nothing written (room 1). Now each of them shakes one more hand, and the two newcomers meet each other.

A handshake with a newcomer is a copy. The newcomer holds the promise too. Nothing is written, and the step can still be undone.

A handshake between two holders is a chance. Each makes one light touch. Usually it lands nowhere. Sometimes it lands on a line, with a chance set by that line's length.

The first time both touches land on the same line, that line is written for every holder at once. Length times length: the square. Nobody proposes, compares or refuses. A miss leaves nothing behind, and the number of misses before the write is the same on every table, so even someone counting them would learn nothing.

A line is written at the first handshake where two holders' touches land on the same line. The crowd makes the chances, not the choice: its wiring decides when and where, never the odds.

Other crowds (a lone thread that never writes, a ring, a room of friends) and other ways of putting the two touches together are under Variations below the picture. They change when and where the write comes, never the odds.

Told to the toy. The touch itself: first order in the line's length, and fresh each time. No visible meeting can make such a touch, because every event in the visible tables comes out squared; so on the toy it is read from each particle's hidden clock, which must be chaotic. Here a fixed-seed generator stands in for the clocks. Where the touch comes from belongs to another episode. And the laptop plays one handshake at a time: what this room shows is the order of events and the odds, not how long they take.
room 3

One write, then copies

measured on the toy
presets

Once a line is written, witnesses meet the settled particle. What can their meeting do?

Copy. A witness that meets a particle whose line is already written sees a table with one line left, so it can only copy. The odds stay the square, however many witnesses come.

Re-sign. If every witness rolled its own lottery on the whole table, it would contradict the books already written, about one link in three.

Vote. If all books had to agree before anything counts, the longer line would win almost always. That is majority rule, and the square is gone by the third book. It is also how a folk tale spreads: the catchiest version wins.

There is one write, at the first match. Every later meeting copies. Spread buys permanence, not odds.

room 4

Permanence is reach

measured on the toy
presets

To undo a fact, you must undo every meeting that copied it, last one first. That means reaching every book that holds it: P's, M's and each witness's.

Give the undo a chance to reach each book (the "reach" dial). It undoes the fact only if it reaches them all.

The chance of an undo falls geometrically with the books that hold the fact. That is the shape of Nakamoto's catch-up odds: every confirmation multiplies the attacker's chance by the same factor.

Nothing forbids the undo. It just stops being reachable.

room 5

Evidence adds up; hearsay doesn't

derived
presets

Simple form first. Each copy takes with some chance ("copy takes"). The fact can be erased only in the branch where nobody knows it.

A crowd: M tells three friends directly. Everyone fails only if all three fail: at 3 in 4, one in 64. A chain: M tells W1, W1 tells W2, W2 tells W3. If W1 failed, everyone after is silent: one in 4, however long the chain.

The chart shows the corresponding wave calculation: a witness's look turns it a little, and the fringe contrast recoverable before detection is set by the looks nobody undid. With cos(look) equal to the chance a copy fails in the counting analogy, the numbers are the same; the wave calculation retains both alternatives.

With a reach. Now let the undo reach each witness with some chance ("undo reaches") and undo, last first, every look it reaches. From a crowd, what comes back still falls by the same factor per witness: geometric, room 4's shape. From a chain it levels off after a few links. Missing a link costs only what the shallowest missed link costs; deeper misses cost nothing more.

A factor per unreached direct witness, and the factors multiply. A witness of a witness adds a listener, not a look. Evidence adds up; hearsay doesn't.

This is why a Bitcoin confirmation counts: every block re-hashes the history itself, a fresh direct look, not a forwarded opinion. Gossip that only forwards is spread without finality.

Two threads. After P meets D, P's later meetings and D's later meetings are two sub-trees of the fact. Each sub-tree costs the undo its own factor, and inside each, only the shallowest missed link counts.

Proved on the toy. The shallowest-link rule, the crowd's product and both averages are exact identities for chains and crowds of any length (the message box prints the formulas). Each unreached direct witness is worth the same amount of finality: half a bit for a 45° look.

The shallowest-link rule and the crowd's product are a machine-checked proof (Lean 4, Mathlib) for any number of witnesses. One honest clause: it is proved for a line-by-line copy of the toy's code in Lean, which reproduces every number the toy ever stored; for looks between 0° and 90°.

Independent witnesses only. The product and "deeper misses cost nothing more" hold for witnesses that start clean and separate. Witnesses that already share a record along the look's direction count as one look of the summed angle, and a matched pair can hide the look completely. The product is the independent case of that more general law.
room 6

Where the write sits is invisible

measured on the toy
presets

P has met M, and a witness W has copied M. Now someone turns P and reads it. Two ways to predict the reading:

The whole table. Nothing is ever cut; just run the turn on the joint table of P, M and W.

The narrowed table. Pretend a match already wrote one line, then turn that line alone, and average.

As long as the move misses at least one book that holds the fact, the two predictions agree exactly. Narrowing is conditioning on the books beyond reach. The table never narrows.

So nobody who misses a witness can tell where, or whether, the line was written. Only a move that reaches every book sees the difference: the fringe comes back.

room 7

A fact for which reading?

measured on the toy
presets

A witness copies P's line and goes away. Later somebody reads P, straight or after a turn. Does the copy matter?

Compare the reading with the copy and without it. After a straight reading the two agree exactly: the copy told the world nothing the reading would not have said anyway. After a turn they differ: the copy has removed the fringe the turn would have shown.

A copy is a fact only for a reading that could contradict it. The same entry is a fact under one later turn and a dead letter under another, and the late choice signals nothing.

On the toy, more than half of what the vacuum writes is a fact for nobody in this sense (55.6% on the meter).

room 8

The cat is a fork

measured on the toy
steps

Inside a sealed box: an atom A, a detector D, the cat C. Outside: you, O. Step through the meetings.

The atom meets the detector, the detector meets the cat: copies. Inside the box the holders keep meeting, and the first match writes the line, somewhere in the box (room 2). The cat's book agrees with the detector's completely. Across the box wall there is nothing: your book knows nothing about the cat.

Opening the box is your first link: your book copies the cat's.

Decided inside the box, unreceived until your first link. "Alive and dead" is the outside's table, not the cat. Opening the box does not create the fact; it delivers it.

What is still installed

What the toy is still told, one item per open question about the settlement. Everything else on this page follows from meetings.

  • result forced · touch installed The touch. Which meetings settle now follows from meetings: any meeting of two holders is a chance (room 2). What the toy is still told is the touch: first order in the line's length, and fresh each time. A touch with memory, or a pick that waits for a match, misses the square. No visible meeting can make such a touch, because every event in the visible tables comes out squared; so the touch lives below the waterline, read from each particle's hidden clock. Its misses leave no record, and their count is the same on every table, so it reads nobody's setting.
  • set by the touch How fast a line is written. About π²/(4ε²) chances after the record reaches a second holder, where ε is the touch's strength: fifteen at the bench's 0.4. Nature's strength is not known. The laptop plays one meeting at a time; the bench shows the order of events (copies first, then the first match, never earlier), not how long it takes.
  • checked on the toy Spare particles. The split into equal cells needs spare particles. On the toy they always exist and change no odds. For nature it is assumed that a fresh particle is always available.
  • claimed harmless An undo is out of reach, never impossible. Textbook collapse is absolute; in the copy models, a joint undo that reaches every book restores interference before the final readout (rooms 4 and 6). We claim no real undo reaches every link of a real crowd. That claim is not measured yet.
  • not emerged Which line. Meetings fix the odds, not the line. The line the books land on is the hidden variable, and it belongs to another episode.
  • Also told: the dial angles and the prepared word. Every curve here is standard quantum mechanics (decoherence, the eraser, envariance). What the piece adds: the write at the first match among holders, and the reach law read as consensus.

What this bench does not claim. Rooms 1, 5, 6, 7 and 8 are standard quantum mechanics on a few qubits (decoherence, the quantum eraser, Wigner's friend); rooms 3 and 4 are counting. Room 2 is the toy's own settlement: its odds are the square, as in quantum mechanics, but the touch is put in by hand, and its picks come from a fixed-seed generator standing in for hidden clocks. The neighbours are Zurek (decoherence, envariance, quantum Darwinism), Ollivier, Poulin and Zurek, Rovelli's relative facts, Cramer's offer and confirmation, and Nakamoto 2008, section 11. What the bench adds is the ledger picture. It does not produce which line comes true. Line 1 carries a fixed phase of 60°; any phase gives the same results. Random counts use a fixed seed, so a rerun prints the same numbers.
